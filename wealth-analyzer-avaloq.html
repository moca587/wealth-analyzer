Score methodology: 100 = all growth assets in Roth/ISA/TFSA (fully tax-free). Tax-deferred accounts score 70% (gains grow untaxed but withdrawals are taxed as income). Taxable accounts score 0%. Annual drag = taxable balance × portfolio return × 25% assumed marginal rate. Shortfall cost-basis logic: when a cash-flow shortfall occurs, the engine assumes the gap is funded from a taxable account at 100% cost basis, adding a tax cost of shortfall × effective rate.
Mean-Variance Optimization — Efficient Frontier
Harry Markowitz's Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT) plots every known risk profile on the return vs. volatility plane. The efficient frontier shows the maximum expected return achievable for each level of risk. Your current household profile is highlighted — any point above the curve is unattainable; points below are sub-optimal.
Return profiles are sourced from long-run institutional capital market assumptions. Each dot represents the expected arithmetic mean return (μ) and annualised standard deviation (σ) for that allocation style. The efficient frontier curve is fitted through these points — your household profile is marked with a larger dot. Move right on the x-axis to accept more risk for higher expected returns.

Stochastic engine — what happens each year
Investment return is sampled log-normally via Box–Muller as (μ − 0.5σ²) + σ × N(0,1). Property appreciates by a random 3% ± 2% each year. Income grows by your raise %, expenses grow by inflation. Each loan amortizes with its real interest math. When the year ends with a positive surplus, 30% goes to investments and 70% to cash. Initial investment allocation is max(actual investment-account balances, 30% of total account assets). Net worth at each step is cash + investments + property + other − remaining loan balances.
How goals are scored
For each goal, the engine counts the share of simulation paths whose net worth meets or exceeds the target dollar amount in the goal's calendar year (targetYear − currentYear).
What the simulation does not currently model
The Retirement Planning panel (lifestyle, age, spending, location), goal category and priority, time-horizon dropdown, children, and address fields are stored but do not influence the projection. There is no retirement drawdown phase, tax modelling, social-security benefit estimator, currency conversion, portfolio rebalancing, or sensitivity analysis. Returns assume a static allocation across the entire horizon.

🎯 Goals-Based Wealth Analysis
Institutional goals-based framework modeled on the Merrill Lynch Wealth Management Analysis. Each goal is priced in present-value terms, discounted at a risk-adjusted rate matched to its priority, and compared to your funding sources to produce a Funding Ratio. Uses per-asset-class capital market assumptions with a full correlation matrix for accurate portfolio risk.

Potentially Achievable Annual Lifestyle
Estimated sustainable annual retirement spending (today's dollars) that lasts to age 90, under three confidence levels. 80% is the conservative/pessimistic floor (returns below mean); 30% is optimistic (returns above mean).
Wealth Allocation Framework
Merrill's three-bucket framework organizes assets by risk-return role: Personal (preserve lifestyle — residence, cash), Market (balance risk & return — diversified equities/bonds), and Aspirational (idiosyncratic upside — concentrated stock, business, crypto, investment property).
Capital Market Assumptions
Per-asset-class long-term return/risk assumptions used to derive your portfolio's risk-adjusted return. Geometric mean = arithmetic − ½σ². Core long-term inflation assumption: 2.28%.
Educational projection only — not financial advice. Funding ratios and achievable lifestyle figures are hypothetical, derived from capital market assumptions and your inputs. Actual results will differ. Consult a licensed advisor.
