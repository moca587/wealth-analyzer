Simulation Settings
remainder in cash
Two-bucket model: the simulation splits all wealth into an invested bucket (earns your return mean ± volatility each year) and a cash bucket (flat — no growth, but accumulates annual savings). This % sets how much stays invested. When rebalancing is on, the split is restored each year or whenever drift exceeds 5%. Has no effect if rebalancing is set to None.

Methodology & Disclaimer
Inputs the simulation reads
Income (sum of all four income fields), annual raise %, base expenses (Living + Insurance + Other), inflation rate (taken from the Region preset — 50-yr historical CPI averages — or your manual override), property value, other assets, the sum of every entry on the Assets tab, and each loan's balance / rate / remaining years. Projection length and number of simulations come from this tab.

When two clients are in the household, the mean and volatility are averaged.
Stochastic engine — what happens each year
Investment return is sampled log-normally via Box–Muller as (μ − 0.5σ²) + σ × N(0,1). Property appreciates by a random 3% ± 2% each year. Income grows by your raise %, expenses grow by inflation. Each loan amortizes with its real interest math. When the year ends with a positive surplus, 30% goes to investments and 70% to cash. Initial investment allocation is max(actual investment-account balances, 30% of total account assets). Net worth at each step is cash + investments + property + other − remaining loan balances.
How goals are scored
For each goal, the engine counts the share of simulation paths whose net worth meets or exceeds the target dollar amount in the goal's calendar year (targetYear − currentYear).
What the simulation does not currently model
The Retirement Planning panel (lifestyle, age, spending, location), goal category and priority, time-horizon dropdown, children, and address fields are stored but do not influence the projection. There is no retirement drawdown phase, tax modelling, social-security benefit estimator, currency conversion, portfolio rebalancing, or sensitivity analysis. Returns assume a static allocation across the entire horizon.
Disclaimer. This tool is for educational and planning purposes only. It is not investment, tax, or legal advice. Historical averages and randomly sampled returns do not predict actual market behaviour; real outcomes can differ materially due to sequence-of-returns risk, tax drag, behavioural decisions, fees, and unmodeled life events. Consult a licensed financial professional before acting on any projection shown here.

Client Wealth Report (PDF)
Generate a professional multi-section PDF covering household profile, income & tax, assets & liabilities, goals & retirement, Monte Carlo projection results, cash-flow analysis, funding status, and a full page of regulatory disclosures. Run the simulation at least once before generating the report so projection results and funding status appear in the PDF.
Report Contents
1. Cover page — title, prepared-for/by, date, confidentiality notice
2. Executive summary — net worth, monthly surplus, total assets, household risk, goal success rate
3. Household profile — primary and partner (if added), children, country, risk profile, time horizon
4. Income, expenses & tax — gross income by client, household expenses, computed income tax and bracket details, after-tax net income
5. Assets & liabilities — every account, property, other assets, each loan with amortization
6. Goals & retirement plan — all goals with target year, retirement age/spend, pension source
7. Portfolio analysis — holdings, asset-class mix, diversification, weighted expected return
8. Monte Carlo projection — simulation parameters, median / 30-80 bands over horizon, final-year percentile table
9. Funding status — each goal and retirement at 30 / 50 / 80% confidence with probability-funded
10. Cash-flow projection — year-by-year to age 90 (or 5-yr summary), income / pension / tax / expenses / net worth
11. Methodology & assumptions — Box-Muller log-normal engine, inflation model, tax drag on returns
12. Important disclosures — not advice, historical averages, no guarantees, sequence risk, tax caveats, conflicts, regulatory scope
